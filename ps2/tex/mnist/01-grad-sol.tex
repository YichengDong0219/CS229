\begin{answer}

        Gradient $\frac{\partial l_{CE}(t, y)}{\partial t}$: \\

        \[ l_{CE}(t, y) = -\log(\exp(t_y)) + \log(\sum_k \exp(t_k)) = -t_{y} + \log(\sum_k \exp(t_k)) \]
        The partial derivative of the second term w.R.t. a single logit $f_j$ is:
        \[ \frac{\partial}{\partial t_j} \log(\sum_k \exp(t_k)) =  \frac{1}{\sum_k \exp(t_k)} \cdot \exp(t_j) = \frac{\exp(t_j)}{\sum_k \exp(t_k)} \]
        
        The expression for the derivative is same as the definition of $p_j$, the softmax of the jth output of t - the logits! So we have:
        \[ \frac{\partial}{\partial t} (\log(\sum_k \exp(t_k))) = p \]
        The first expression of the loss gradient, $t_y$, is depending on the specific component $t_j$ we are derivating with respect to. For $j=y$, the derivative would be -1, else zero. \\

        For the derivative of the the loss first loss term w.r.t the complete vector $t$, we get:
\[ \frac{\partial}{\partial t} (-t_y) = [0, \dots, 1, \dots, 0]^T \quad \text{with 1 at position y} \]
This is the one-hot on vector \(e_y\). Putting both term derivatives together,
\[ \frac{\partial L_{CE}(t,y)}{\partial t} = p - e_y \]
\end{answer}
