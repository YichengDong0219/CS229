To prove i) is part of the exponential family, we need to prove it follows this form:
\[ p(y; \eta) = b(y) \exp(\eta^T T(y) - a(\eta)) \]

With $\lambda^y = e^{\log(\lambda^y)} = e^{y \log(\lambda)}$, we can say:
\begin{align*}
    p(y; \lambda) &= \frac{1}{y!} \exp(y \log(\lambda) - \lambda) \\
    &= \underbrace{\frac{1}{y!}}_{b(y)} \exp(\underbrace{y}_{T(y)} \underbrace{\log(\lambda)}_{\eta^T} - \underbrace{\lambda}_{a(\eta)})
\end{align*}
As $a(\eta) = \lambda$ is not a function of $\eta$, we can rewrite it as $a(\eta) = e^\eta$ \\ (with $\log(\lambda) = \eta \rightarrow \lambda=e^\eta$) \\


So we have:
\begin{align*}
    b(y) &= \frac{1}{y!} \\
    T(y) &= y \\
    \eta &= \log(\lambda) \\
    a(\eta) &= e^\eta
\end{align*}
\end{enumerate}
