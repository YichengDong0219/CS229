\begin{answer}

    K=2 (Binary classification)

Let $p$ be the proportion of training examples of class 1 \\
Let $(1-p)$ be the proportion of training examples of class 2 \\

\textbf{Show: The Gini loss is a strictly concave function:}

Gini loss for a Region R with p as proportion of class 1:
\[ G(p) = p(1-p) + (1-p)(1-(1-p)) = p-p^2+p-p^2 = 2p-2p^2 \]
To check for concavity, we need to check whether the second derivative is smaller or equal to 0.
\begin{align*}
    \frac{dG}{dp} &= 2-4p \\
    \frac{d^2 G}{dp^2} &= -4
\end{align*}
$\rightarrow G(p)$ is strictly concave for $p \in (0,1)$ \\

\textbf{Parent \& Child Proportion Relation}

Let $R_p$ be the parent region, with children $R_1$ and $R_2$
$\rightarrow$ 

Let $N_p, N_1, N_2$ be the number of data points in $R_p, R_1, R_2$, so that $N_p = N_1 + N_2$.

Let $p_p, p_1, p_2$ be the proportion of class 1 examples in $R_p, R_1, R_2$.

With every positive example of the parent going to one of the children, we have:
\begin{align*}
    N_p \cdot p_p &= N_1 \cdot p_1 + N_2 \cdot p_2 \\
    \Rightarrow p_p &= \frac{N_1}{N_p} \cdot p_1 + \frac{N_2}{N_p} \cdot p_2
\end{align*}
With $t = \frac{N_1}{N_p}$, and $(1-t) = \frac{N_2}{N_p}$ as the relative amounts of examples in each child set, we get:
\[ p_p = t \cdot p_1 + (1-t) \cdot p_2 \]

With the inequalities in the text, we get
\[ G(p_p) > t \cdot G(p_1) + (1-t) G(p_2) \]
With $t=\frac{N_1}{N_p}$ and $(1-t)=\frac{N_2}{N_p}$ we get:
\[ G(p_p) > \frac{N_1}{N_p} G(R_1) + \frac{N_2}{N_p} G(R_2) \]
The total number of examples in each space equals the size of the spaces $R_p, R_1$ and $R_2$.
\[ G(R_p) > \frac{|R_1| \cdot G(R_1) + |R_2| \cdot G(R_2)}{|R_p|} \]
\end{answer}
